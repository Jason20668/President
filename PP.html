His Presidency

Events of his Presidency:

On June 6th, 1856, the Democratic Party chooses Buchanan as their Representative and he wins the Presidency on September 4th, 1856. He gives his Inaugural Address on slavery on March 4th of 1857 and 2 days later the Dred Scott decision is announced. He submiots the Lecompton Consitution on February 2nd, 1858 but it is defeted by Kansas 6 months later and fails for its 2nd time. During his Presidency 3 new states are ratified those being Kansas, Minnesota, and Oregon.

Challenges During His Presidency:

During his time in office, Buchanan faced several major challenges. His 1st challenge was the Dred Scott decision which he supported to the joy and anger of many. He also had to deal with the Panic of 1857. He chose to be hands-off in his approach and it led to the panic going on for a year and a half. He also had to deal with Bleeding Kansas, violent confrontations between people in Nebraska and Kansas over whether or not to allow slavery. He decided to support the Lecompton Constitution which was pro-slavery rather than try and mitigate the fighting. His also had to deal with Harper's Ferry Raid where an abolitionist tried and failed to raid a federal arsenal. While Buchanan condemned the raid his presidency was critisized for not stopping the underlying problems. His final challenge was the secession of multiple states from the Union such as South Carolina and many other Southern States. Buchanan did effectively nothing to stop this as he felt the federal governemnt didn't have the abiltiy to.

Legacy:

Ultimately, Buchanan's legacy as President is considered to be one of, if not the worst. His inability and unwillingness to stop major events from happening or prevent the reasons for these events happening lead to a very critisized presidency. He not only refused to support the people during a recession, he also refused to do anything when states started to secede or mitigate the violent nature attached to pro vs. anti slavery debate.
